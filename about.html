Context, Purpose & Partners

Why OLA Exists:
Human Capital for Africa’s Future

Nigeria will account for 1-of-10 global births by 2050. Odiso Leadership Academy (OLA) is built to ensure deserving Nigerian girls are tooled to lead this transformation.

The Grounding Data

Nigeria’s Human Capital Challenge & Geostrategic Promise

With over 230 million people and a median age of 19, Nigeria is the epicenter of UNICEF’s forecast that “the future of humanity is increasingly African.” By 2050, 1 in 4 global workers will be African.

Yet Nigeria is constrained by severe human capital challenges: ranked 161 in the World Bank 2020 Human Capital Index, it has the world’s second highest number of persons living in extreme poverty, and the world’s largest out-of-school population—over 15 million children, disproportionately girls.

The OLA Solution

A Post-Secondary Pre-Tertiary Venture

Odiso Leadership Academy (OLA) is the sole not-for-profit non-parochial private education equity school in Nigeria equipping the most promising girls from all 36 states to compete in science, technology, engineering, arts, agriculture, and mathematics (STEAAM).

✓ Advancing UN SDG #4 (Quality Education) & SDG #5 (Gender Equality)
Institutional Alliances

Collaborating with Global Academic Leaders

Securing dual enrollment acceleration, college credit transfer, and gold-standard evaluation.

Carnegie Mellon University (CMU)

School of Computer Science & College of Fine Arts

OLA is collaborating with CMU’s world-renowned faculties to allow students to take courses that transfer and count as equivalent to college credits. This proof of concept is expanding to CMU-Africa College of Engineering and leading Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs).

Dual Enrollment Credit Transfer Model

Columbia University Teachers College

Edmund Gordon Institute (IUME)

Enlisted the Edmund Gordon Institute for Urban and Minority Education (IUME) at Columbia University to enrich, monitor, and evaluate OLA’s capacity to administer college-level courses in strict alignment with international accreditation standards.

Rigorous Monitoring & Evaluation Framework
2026 Pilot & Sustainable Multiplier

The 2026 Pilot of 15 Scholars & The STEAAM Teachers Corps

As a learning organization, OLA begins in 2026 with a pilot of 15 resident scholars at its Lagos Free Zone campus in Ibeju-Lekki.

Upon completing two years of intensive pre-tertiary STEAAM preparation, students transfer their college-equivalent credits to accelerate their bachelor’s degrees abroad.

The Service & Leadership Accord

“Beneficiaries sign a binding agreement with OLA to return to Nigeria after studying abroad to serve in the OLA STEAAM Teachers Corps (fulfilling their NYSC obligation) and undertake graduate studies in STEAAM to mentor the next generation.”
